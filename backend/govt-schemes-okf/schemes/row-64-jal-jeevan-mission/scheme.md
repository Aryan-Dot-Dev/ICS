# Jal Jeevan Mission

## Overview

Jal Jeevan Mission (JJM) is a flagship program of the Government of India aimed at providing safe and adequate drinking water through individual household tap connections to all rural households by 2024, with an extension approved up to December 2028 under JJM 2.0. The mission emphasizes community participation, source sustainability, greywater management, and information, education, and communication (IEC) as key components. It aims to create a 'Jan Andolan' for water, making it everyone's priority, and ensures functionality of tap connections through regular monitoring and community ownership.

## Objective

- Provide Functional Tap Connection (FHTC) to every rural household
- Prioritize FHTC provision in quality-affected, drought-prone, desert areas, and Sansad Adarsh Gram Yojana (SAGY) villages
- Provide functional tap connections to schools, Anganwadi centres, GP buildings, health centres, wellness centres, and community buildings
- Monitor functionality of tap connections
- Promote voluntary ownership through community contribution in cash, kind, labour, or shramdaan
- Ensure sustainability of water supply systems (source, infrastructure, funds for O&M)
- Empower and develop human resources in the water sector for construction, plumbing, electrical, water quality management, treatment, catchment protection, O&M, etc.
- Create awareness on safe drinking water and stakeholder involvement to make water everyone's business

## Eligibility

States and Union Territories (UTs) are eligible to receive financial assistance under Jal Jeevan Mission for planning, implementing, and managing rural water supply schemes. ## Important Conditions

- Funds are released subject to utilization of previous installments and submission of required documents
- Community contribution is mandatory for sustainability
- Extension of JJM up to December 2028 is under JJM 2.0 with structural reforms
- Focus has shifted from infrastructure creation to citizen-centric service delivery and functionality
- Digital monitoring and local governance are critical for success
