# Transit Oriented Development (TOD) Policy

## Overview

The Urban Challenge Fund (UCF) is a centrally sponsored scheme under the Ministry of Housing and Urban Affairs (MoHUA) designed to catalyze market-based financing for urban infrastructure projects. It provides Central Assistance (CA) as catalytic support to leverage larger investments from bonds, bank loans, and public-private partnerships (PPPs). The fund supports projects across three verticals: 'Cities as Growth Hubs', 'Creative Redevelopment of Cities', and 'Water and Sanitation', with a focus on Transit-Oriented Development (TOD) infrastructure development as an admissible project type.

## Objective

- Position cities as a bankable asset class through targeted reforms
- Strengthen institutional capacity and improve governance and planning frameworks
- Develop sustainable revenue-generating urban infrastructure
- Encourage transition towards market-based financing and integrated regional development
- Leverage significantly larger investments from market-based sources using Central Assistance as catalytic support
- Support projects under the three verticals: Cities as Growth Hubs, Creative Redevelopment of Cities, and Water and Sanitation
- Promote Transit-Oriented Development (TOD) infrastructure development for planned densification around transit hubs
- Ensure financially viable and sustainable urban infrastructure investments across the project life cycle

## Important Conditions

- Central Assistance for projects is limited to 25% of the cost of bankable projects
- At least 50% of the project cost must be funded from bonds, bank loans, and PPPs
- Fund release is contingent upon utilization of previous tranches (70% utilization required for next tranche)
- States must endeavour to take up projects equivalent to at least 25% of their allocated amount within six months from issuance of guidelines
- After two years, State/UT-wise progress will be reviewed by the National Apex Committee (NAC)
- Projects must be designed as part of a comprehensive and may affect future allocations
