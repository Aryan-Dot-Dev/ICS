## Process

1. Identify prospective beneficiaries from the urban poor through Community Organisers (COs) and ULB professionals.
2. Community structures like Self Help Groups (SHGs) and Area Level Federations (ALFs) may refer beneficiaries for financial assistance.
3. Beneficiaries may directly approach ULB or its representatives, or banks may identify and send cases to ULB.
4. Submit an application of intent on plain paper with basic details: Name, Age, Contact details, Address, Aadhaar (if any), loan amount required, bank account number (if available), type of enterprise/activity, and category.
5. ULB enters details in a register or MIS and generates a waiting list with a unique registration number for tracking.
6. ULB conducts awareness campaigns through mass media, IEC, local newspapers, and City Livelihood Centres (CLCs).
7. ULB calls beneficiaries in waiting list order to complete documentation including Loan Application Form (LAF), activity details, identity proof, address proof, and bank account details.
8. SULM may develop a standardized LAF in consultation with State Level Bankers Committee (SLBC) convenor bank.
9. Completed applications are sent to the ULB-level Task Force for scrutiny.
10. Task Force calls prospective beneficiaries for an interview before recommending or rejecting the application or requesting additional information.
11. Recommended cases are forwarded by ULB to concerned banks for processing within 15 days.
12. Banks send periodic reports to ULB on application status; if MIS is used, banks may update status online in addition to manual reports.
13. For SEP-G (Group Enterprises), minimum 5 members with at least 70% from urban poor families are required, and no more than one person from the same family should be included in the group.
14. For SEP-I (Individual Enterprises), applicant must be at least 18 years of age.
15. For SHG Bank Linkage, SHGs must have at least 70% urban poor members and follow group norms.
16. For EST&P, candidates undergo skill gap analysis, curriculum design aligned with NSQF, and training of minimum 200 hours for fresh entrants or 16 hours for reskilling.
17. For PMFME convergence seed capital, SHG members engaged in micro food processing with at least one year experience apply via hard copy forms (Form I & II) facilitated by ROs/COs/CRPs, including enterprise details, product category, turnover, bank passbook, FSSAI/GST certificates if available, and photograph of enterprise.
18. Applications are scrutinized by SHG in meetings, forwarded to ULB for approval and digitization in NULM MIS.
19. ULB uploads approved applications to NULM MIS portal after Aadhaar authentication and mobile number validation.
20. SULM recommends applications to State Nodal Agency (SNA) for approval.
21. SNA approves and sanctions amount, which is transferred to SULM’s bank account.
22. SULM transfers funds to concerned federation’s (ALF/CLF) bank account within 10 days.
23. ALF/CLF disburses seed capital to individual SHG members’ bank accounts or to enterprise bank account for group/activity groups.
24. Beneficiaries utilize funds within one month’s interest-free lock-in period for procurement and installation.
25. ULBs collect fund utilization details through ROs/COs/CRPs and enter data via ULB login.
26. EMI repayment is supported by ROs/COs/CRPs, with ALF recovering EMI through SHGs and forwarding monthly reports to ULB.
27. For EST&P, candidates are mobilized, allotted to training centres, and training is conducted with attendance tracking.
28. Post-training, candidates are forwarded to Certifying Agency (CA) for assessment, and placement details are recorded.
29. For City Livelihood Centres (CLCs), ULB prepares Detailed Implementation Plan (DIP) after survey, ensures land ownership, relocation plan if any, infrastructure details, beneficiary list, and safety concerns.
30. DIP is submitted to SLAC for approval, and funds are released in installments contingent on beneficiary list submission.
31. For Shelter for Urban Homeless (SUH), ULB or other agencies operate shelters with minimum staff: one full-time manager and three caregivers (one per 8-hour shift), with at least one woman caregiver for women inmates.
32. For street vendor components, ULB conducts survey, issues identity cards, prepares city street vending plan, improves infrastructure, provides training (1-2 days with stipend not less than minimum wage), promotes financial inclusion, facilitates access to credit, and links to social security schemes.
33. All components require monitoring and evaluation through MIS, monthly progress reports, and quarterly performance tracking.

## Deadlines

- Rolling basis — applications are accepted throughout the year.
