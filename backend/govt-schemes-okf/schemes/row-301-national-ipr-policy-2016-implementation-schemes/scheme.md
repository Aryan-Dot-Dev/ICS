# National IPR Policy 2016 Implementation Schemes

## Overview

The National IPR Policy 2016 Implementation Schemes, administered by the Office of the Controller General of Patents, Designs and Trade Marks (CGPDTM) under the Department for Promotion of Industry and Internal Trade (DPIIT), Ministry of Commerce and Industry, aim to strengthen India's intellectual property ecosystem through awareness, facilitation, and protection mechanisms. These schemes support innovators, startups, MSMEs, and creators across various IP domains including patents, designs, trademarks, geographical indications, and copyrights by providing procedural guidance, fee structures, examination facilities, and recognition frameworks.

## Objective

- Promote IPR awareness and creation across sectors
- Facilitate filing and prosecution of IP applications
- Provide procedural and fee-related guidance for IP protection
- Support innovation through IP asset creation and commercialization
- Strengthen institutional capacity for IP administration


## Target Beneficiaries

**startups**, **MSME**, **entrepreneurs**, **individual inventors**, **educational institutions**

## Eligibility

Eligibility varies by IP type but generally includes natural persons, startups, small entities, educational institutions, and other legal entities applying for patents, designs, trademarks, geographical indications, or copyrights under the respective Acts and Rules, with specific fee concessions available for startups and small entities as per the First Schedule of the Patents Rules 2003 and…

## Benefits

Benefits include legal protection of intellectual property, exclusive rights to exploit creations, facilitation through e-filing systems, access to examination and publication facilities, fee concessions for startups and small entities, certificate issuance, journal publication, and support for international applications via PCT and Madrid Protocol, along with awareness programs and training…

## Important Conditions

- Fee concessions for startups and small entities require proper declaration and eligibility verification
- Applications may be rejected for incomplete forms, insufficient fees, or lack of novelty/originality
- Geographical Indication applications require detailed documentation including maps, affidavits, and producer details
- Trademark opposition can be filed by third parties within 4 months of publication in the Trade Marks Journal
- Patent renewal fees increase over time and must be paid to maintain protection
- The information provided on workflow pages is for guidance only and does not constitute legal advice
