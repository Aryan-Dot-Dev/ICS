# Electricity Amendment Act – Open Access for MSMEs

## Overview

The Electricity Amendment Act – Open Access for MSMEs is a policy initiative aimed at enabling Micro, Small, and Medium Enterprises (MSMEs) to access electricity through open access mechanisms, allowing them to procure power directly from generators or traders rather than being restricted to local distribution companies. This scheme promotes competition, reduces electricity costs, and enhances energy choice for MSMEs by facilitating their participation in open access transactions under the Electricity Act, 2003. It supports the broader goal of making the power sector more efficient, transparent, and consumer-centric.

## Objective

- Enable MSMEs to access electricity through open access mechanisms
- Reduce electricity costs for MSMEs by promoting competition in power procurement
- Enhance energy choice and reliability for MSME consumers
- Promote transparency and efficiency in the power sector
- Support the integration of renewable energy by enabling MSMEs to source green power
- Facilitate ease of doing business for MSMEs in the electricity domain

## Eligibility

Micro, Small, and Medium Enterprises (MSMEs) as defined under the MSME Development Act, 2006, seeking to procure electricity through open access under the Electricity Act, 2003, and meeting the technical and regulatory requirements set by the Central Electricity Regulatory Commission (CERC) or State Electricity Regulatory Commissions (SERCs) for open access consumers.


Deterministic rules: [eligibility.md](eligibility.md).


## Benefits

MSMEs can procure electricity directly from power generators or through open access, potentially reducing electricity costs through competitive pricing; access to renewable energy sources; improved power supply reliability; freedom to choose power suppliers; and enhanced operational flexibility in energy management.

## Important Conditions

- Open access approval is subject to availability of transmission capacity and grid constraints
- MSMEs must bear all transmission charges, losses, and applicable surcharges as determined by CERC/SERC
- Cross-subsidy surcharge and additional surcharge may apply as per state-specific open access regulations
- Approval process may vary across states and is governed by respective State Electricity Regulatory Commissions (SERCs)
- The scheme does not provide financial subsidies;
- cost benefits depend on market prices and negotiation
