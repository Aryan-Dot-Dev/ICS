# MSME Pre-Pack Insolvency Resolution Process (PPIRP)

## Overview

The MSME Pre-Pack Insolvency Resolution Process (PPIRP) is a streamlined insolvency resolution mechanism under the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code, 2016, designed specifically for Micro, Small, and Medium Enterprises (MSMEs) to facilitate faster and cost-effective resolution of financial distress. It allows the corporate debtor to initiate a resolution plan with the consent of its creditors, bypassing the need for a public invitation of resolution plans, thereby reducing time and cost. The process is governed by the IBBI (Pre-packaged Insolvency Resolution Process) Regulations, 2021, and subsequent amendments, aiming to preserve business continuity and maximize value for stakeholders in the MSME sector.

## Objective

- Facilitate faster resolution of insolvency for MSMEs
- Reduce the cost and time associated with the Corporate Insolvency Resolution Process (CIRP)
- Enable the corporate debtor to initiate resolution with creditor consent
- Preserve business continuity and maximize value for stakeholders
- Provide a mechanism for early identification and exclusion of non-viable projects
- Strengthen project-wise ring-fencing of funds and accounts in real estate insolvency (as part of broader IBC framework)
- Ensure timely handover of possession to allottees in real estate projects
- Enhance transparency and accountability of insolvency professionals

## Key Features

- Geographic scope: Pan-India (central-level)
- Deadline: The application for initiation of PPIRP must be filed before the Adjudicating Authority after the resolution plan is approved by the Committee of Creditors. There is no fixed window; the process can be initiated at any time subject to eligibility. However, once initiated, the resolution process must be completed within 90 days from the date of application, extendable by the Adjudicating Authority for a further 60 days in exceptional circumstances, making a maximum of 150 days.

## Important Conditions

- PPIRP is not available if a corporate insolvency resolution process has already been initiated under section 7, 9, or 10 of the Code
- The process cannot be used if an order for liquidation has been passed
- A corporate debtor cannot undergo PPIRP more than once in a period of three years
- The resolution plan must comply with section 30(2) of the Code, including payment of CIRP costs and operational creditor dues in priority
- The successful resolution applicant must be eligible under section 29A of the Code
- The process does not apply to personal guarantors to corporate debtors
- The Adjudicating Authority may reject the application if the resolution plan is not feasible or viable
