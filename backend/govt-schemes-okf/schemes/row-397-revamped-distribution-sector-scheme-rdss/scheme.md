# Revamped Distribution Sector Scheme (RDSS)

## Overview

The Revamped Distribution Sector Scheme (RDSS) is a reform-based and results-linked scheme launched by the Government of India to improve the operational efficiencies and financial sustainability of power distribution companies (DISCOMs) across the country. It aims to reduce Aggregate Technical and Commercial (AT&C) losses to pan-India levels of 12-15% by 2024-25 and eliminate the gap between average cost of supply and average revenue realized (ACS-ARR) by 2024-25. The scheme provides financial assistance to DISCOMs for prepaid smart metering, distribution infrastructure upgradation, and system strengthening works. It is implemented through a central sector scheme with funding support from the Government of India and participation from state governments and DISCOMs.

## Objective

- Reduce Aggregate Technical and Commercial (AT&C) losses to pan-India levels of 12-15% by 2024-25
- Eliminate the gap between average cost of supply and average revenue realized (ACS-ARR) by 2024-25
- Modernize and strengthen distribution infrastructure through upgradation and automation
- Install 250 million prepaid smart meters across the country
- Improve the quality, reliability, and affordability of power supply to consumers
- Enhance the financial viability and operational efficiency of DISCOMs
- Promote renewable energy integration and demand-side management
- Ensure 24x7 power supply to all households, agricultural, commercial, and industrial consumers

## Important Conditions

- Financial assistance is contingent upon achieving pre-defined performance targets related to loss reduction and revenue collection
- Funds are released in tranches linked to milestone achievement and third-party verification
- State governments must provide guarantees for loan repayment and subsidy utilization
- The scheme is subject to mid-term review and possible modification based on performance evaluation
- Non-performing DISCOMs may face suspension or termination of financial assistance
- Convergence with other central and state schemes is encouraged but must avoid duplication of funding
