# Aquifer Mapping & Management Programme

## Overview

The National Aquifer Mapping and Management and Management (NAQUIM) programme, initiated in 2012 under the Ground Water Management and Regulation Plan Scheme (XII plan), is one of the largest aquifer mapping endeavours globally. It aims to support effective groundwater management by delineating aquifers, characterizing their hydrogeological properties, assessing resource availability and quality, preparing aquifer maps, identifying issues, and developing sustainability strategies. The programme has completed mapping of 25 lakh km² mappable area out of 33 lakh km² total geographical area by 2023, with outputs disseminated via www.cgwb.gov.in and www.aims-cgwb.org.

## Objective

- Delineation of aquifers in terms of their horizontal and vertical extent
- Characterization of aquifers in terms of their hydrogeological properties
- Assessment of resource availability and quality
- Preparation of aquifer maps
- Identification and quantification of issues
- Developing management strategies to ensure sustainability of ground water resources
- Providing information in higher granularity with focus on dynamic data like ground water level and quality
- Providing issue-based scientific inputs for ground water management up to panchayat level
- Putting in place a strategy to ensure implementation of recommended strategies

## Eligibility

The NAQUIM programme is implemented by the Central Ground Water Board (CGWB) in coordination with state government counterparts and collaborates with organizations like GSI, NGRI, IMD, NRSC, IISc, IIT, etc. There is no direct application process for external beneficiaries; benefits are delivered through government channels and public dissemination of reports and maps.

## Benefits

Outputs support drinking water source finding and sustainability, site selection for artificial recharge, safe drinking water in arsenic-affected areas, assured irrigation in areas with adequate groundwater potential, implementation of water conservation and AR recommendations, ground water regulation based on NAQUIM recommendations, rejuvenation of springs, and integration into Atal Bhujal…

## Important Conditions

- NAQUIM outputs are for government use and public dissemination;
- no direct financial benefits to individuals or private entities
- Implementation of management strategies depends on state and central government adoption
- NAQUIM 2.0 activities are ongoing with priority areas including water stressed areas, coastal areas, urban agglomerates, springsheds, industrial/mining areas, command areas, deep-seated aquifers, auto-flow areas, and poor ground water quality areas
